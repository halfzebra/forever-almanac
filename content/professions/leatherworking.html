Why this pair costs almost nothing

One guide prices the full 1–300 leatherworking route at about 233 gold if you buy every hide off the auction house, or about 19 gold if you skin it yourself. You're the skinner, so you're on the cheap side of a 200-gold gap.

Skin what you killA rogue kills constantly while leveling. Carry the knife and skin everything skinnable on the way past.
Skinning levels itselfNo crafting step, no vendor mats. The only thing that holds it back is your skill being too low for the creature.
Turn leather into armor kitsArmor Kits are the cheapest skill-ups in the profession and a genuine item: they add armor to your gear.
Wear the restUnlike the priest's tailoring, most of what you craft is leather you can actually equip.

What to do with the junk you craft

Armor Kits aside, a lot of the route makes gear nobody wants. Forever gives it two sinks, and leatherworking is unusually well served by both.

crates
Cured hides beat raw leather. A Journeyman Fabrics crate takes 2 Cured Medium Hide and pays 20 Merchant's Favor; a Journeyman Textiles crate takes 20 Medium Leather and pays 10. Curing hides is a cheap trainer recipe, so cure them before you crate them. Apprentice wants 2 Cured Light Hide, Expert 2 Cured Heavy Hide, Artisan 2 Cured Thick Hide. Turn in to Marcy Baker at Three Corners, western Redridge Mountains, around 9.7, 70.9.
writs
Leatherworking has 29 writs, the second most of any profession. Each drops as a sealed envelope from mobs or chests and asks for one named crafted item, which you bring to a customer marked on your map. 75 reputation at Journeyman, 125 at Expert, 200 at Artisan, plus a coin pouch. Each distinct writ once a day, and the item can't be sitting in your bank when you hand it in.
the rest
Vendor it. You have no enchanter on this character, so there is no disenchant option here. Send genuinely useful greens to the priest instead: she can disenchant them and the dust feeds her enchanting.

Leatherworking, 1 to 300

Materials are per craft. Patterns are from your trainer unless noted; the later ones are auction-house pickups, so watch for them early.

A second guide disagrees with this whole route. It runs Dark Leather and Hillman's gear through the middle, then Wicked Leather Gauntlets and Runic Leather Headband from 235 to 300 instead of the Blue Suede and Frostsaber line. Both are one site's opinion and neither has been verified on live. The parts you can rely on are the Armor Kit steps, which are trainer-taught, cheap, and useful items in their own right. The Runecloth in Runic Leather Bracers is your priest's department — have her save you some.

Specializations at 225

Leatherworking keeps the three branches. Dragonscale makes mail, so it's for the hunter or shaman you don't have; the other two make leather.

Tribal — the rogue-shaped one

Long chain: start with Pratt McGrubben in Feralas, complete the five Wild Leather quests, turn in Master of the Wild Leather to Telonis in Darnassus, then finish with Caryssia Moonhunter in Feralas. Budget roughly 192 Thick Leather, 112 Turtle Scale, 7 Wildvine and 4 Cured Thick Hide.

Elemental

Much shorter: bring 2 each of Heart of Fire, Globe of Water, Core of Earth and Breath of Wind to Sarah Tanner in Searing Gorge. Leather armor made with elemental materials.
This is the shakiest section on the page. The specialization steps come from one site that states plainly they are the Classic versions and have not been verified in Forever. The requirement is given as 225 skill and level 40; one guide listed level 400, which is obviously a typo but shows how thin the sourcing is. Check with Telonis before you farm 192 Thick Leather for a chain that may have changed.
