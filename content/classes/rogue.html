Talents: Combat, and you are on route already

You are 10 with one point spent, which is the cleanest possible start. Method recommends Combat for solo players and Subtlety only if you are grouped; wowhandbook recommends Combat and publishes a route that never needs a respec all the way to 60. You level alone, you skin everything you kill, and Blade Flurry turns multi-mob pulls into faster skinning — Combat it is.

Your first point should be Efficient Strikes. Check that it is. wowhandbook, talentsforever and classicwowforever all start Combat with Efficient Strikes 1/2 at level 10, so this is the one talent in the game that three sites agree on without qualification.

One point a level, 10 to 60

Your weapon choice is a talent decision in Forever, not a preference. Hack and Slash does something different depending on what you are holding: a sword or axe gives a chance at an extra attack, a dagger or fist weapon gives 5% crit, and a mace ignores 15% of the target's armour. That is a five-point investment, so pick swords or axes and stay there — the extra-attack proc is what the rest of this build is shaped around. It also means you should not buy the dagger upgrades the quest tables keep offering you.
Method contradicts itself about daggers, and doesn't resolve it. Its Combat section says to "avoid using Daggers" because Sinister Strike does more damage without them — then offers a dagger swap anyway: move 1/5 Lightning Reflexes and 2/2 Endurance into 3/3 Puncturing Wounds and use Backstab. It also says that if you loot a strong dagger by level 30 you should switch to Subtlety outright, "provided you have the currency to do so". Both statements are on the same page. Take the swap only if a dagger genuinely lands in your lap.
Where the sources fall out, so you know what is actually shaky. The spec itself: Method says Combat solo, wowhandbook says Combat, classicwowforever leads with Assassination, and talentsforever reports Subtlety is what players actually pick — 43% of 53,905 builds, against 33.9% Combat and 23.1% Assassination. That is a popularity count, not a recommendation, and it is worth knowing it exists. On timings: Blade Flurry 32 or 30, Riposte 22 or 21, Coup de Grace 14–16 or 12–14, Hack and Slash 5/5 at 43–46 or 2/5 at 33–34, Improved Slice and Dice at 50–52 or 18–20, and Malice last or first. Final spread is 16/35/0 or 13/38/0. Efficient Strikes cuts energy cost by 5 on two sites, but talentsforever's change log says a 2 November hotfix moved it to 3. And talentsforever reports no agreed order at all for levels 40 to 49, in any rogue spec.

Shank, and the five points that serve it

Shank is new, it is not a talent, and it quietly shapes a third of your build. You learn it at level 24, alongside Mind-numbing Poison.

What it does

An off-hand strike for 50% weapon damage that cannot miss, on a 10 sec cooldown. It costs 5 Energy and returns 15. A second rank at level 44 returns 25.
caveatclassicwowforever's phrasing does not make clear whether that return is net of the cost, and wowhandbook never describes Shank's effect at all. Watch your Energy bar the first time you press it.

Three talents key off it

Flash of Steel (Combat, 5 ranks) — +15 Energy and up to −2 sec cooldown on Shank. That is levels 37–41 of your ladder.
Venom (Assassination) — Shank raises poison damage 30% and application chance 20% for 6 sec.
Boot Knife (Subtlety, 5 ranks) — Shank gains up to a 100% chance to add 2 combo points.
So press Shank on cooldown from 24, and know what you are buying at 37. Five of your 51 points go into an ability most guides mention only in passing. If Shank turns out to feel bad in practice, Flash of Steel is the clean place to cut — which is worth knowing before you commit, because nothing else in the Combat tree depends on it.

What to actually press

wowhandbook and Method agree on this, which given how much else they disagree about is reassuring.

the opener

Stealth, then pick one

  1. Enter Stealth before the pull
  2. Garrote if the target will survive its full duration
  3. Ambush if it won't
Sap no longer breaks Stealth in Forever, per Blizzard's 7 Oct deep dive.
the loop

Sinister Strike, Shank, Riposte

  • Sinister Strike as your builder — Combat "prefers using Sinister Strike over Backstab"
  • Shank on cooldown, from 24
  • Riposte the moment it lights up — 150% weapon damage and a 6 sec disarm
  • Expose Armor only if the target lives its full duration and Slice and Dice is already running
combo points

Slice and Dice before Eviscerate

  • Spend on Slice and Dice first — learned at 10, and it "should be maintained as the better of the two options while levelling"
  • Once Slice and Dice is up, spend on Eviscerate
  • If the next pull is imminent, spend on Slice and Dice anyway so it carries over
cooldowns & poisons

Save them for packs

  • Save Blade Flurry and Adrenaline Rush for packs and dungeon bosses — not single mobs
  • Poisons from 20: Deadly and Instant on your weapons before combat
Method's line on using both together is cut off mid-sentence in its own page, at "when they both become available at Level 30-".
wowhandbook contradicts itself twice, in ways that matter a little. Its intro says "Sinister Strike on every cooldown" while its own rotation says "whenever you have the Energy" — Sinister Strike has no cooldown, so read it as the second. And its intro claims "every point into Combat until 46" while its own table puts 2 points into Assassination at levels 12–13, which is the Remorseless Attacks pick your ladder uses.

Quest rewards worth a detour

Two of these are not optional. The rest are weapons, filtered to what a sword-and-axe Combat rogue actually wants — the dagger picks the guides push are deliberately left out.

Level 20: the Poisons chain. This is a hard gate. Alliance runs Mathias and the Defias (quest 2360) from Mathias Shaw in Stormwind, which sends you to Westfall, then Klaven's Tower (quest 2359). It rewards the Poisons skill itself plus Recipe: Thistle Tea. You cannot make poisons at all until this is done, so put it at the top of your level 20 list.
Level 10, right now, and the sources disagree about whether it exists. Timesaver lists a Blade of Cunning (6.75 DPS) as a rogue class quest reward at 10 for both factions. ConquestCapped's class-quest table lists no level 10 rogue quest at all. You are exactly the right level to settle it — ask your trainer.
One thing not to chase. Timesaver's words on Dragon's Call (41.40 DPS at 55): it is a 1.48% drop, so treat it as "a bonus, not a goal". Same logic applies to anything on these tables under a few percent.
